♜ Coach Rook Open the board

A chess coach that watches your board.

Coach Rook joins you on a video call beside a real board. Solve puzzles or go through one of your own games. The coach sees every move you make, talks it through with you, and points at the squares it means.

Open the board The board is open to anyone. The video coach needs an access code.

Puzzles

About 4,800 positions from the Lichess puzzle database, sorted into 20 tactical themes and three levels. Solve two cleanly and the next one is harder. Struggle and it eases off.

Say what you want out loud: an easier one, a harder one, or a theme by name. When you're stuck, hints come in steps. First a question, then a highlighted piece, then the name of the pattern. You get the answer only when you ask for it.

Game review

Paste a PGN or a Lichess link. Stockfish goes through every move and picks out the ones that cost you the most.

The coach takes you back to the position before each one, asks what you were thinking, and lets you try again on the board. Any move that holds the position counts, and the engine's first choice is only one of them. If you want it, the coach plays the better line out in front of you.

Checked by the engine

The coach doesn't calculate. Before it calls a move good or bad it asks Stockfish, and the board reports each move you play along with what that move allowed.

Points at the board

Highlights and arrows appear on your board while the coach talks, so "this knight" means a square you can see.

Remembers you

Give your name and it keeps a notebook: what you solved, what took a few tries, which mistakes came up in your games. The next session starts from there.

Waits while you think

Turn-taking is set for long silences. If you go quiet for a while you get a nudge, and the answer stays hidden.
